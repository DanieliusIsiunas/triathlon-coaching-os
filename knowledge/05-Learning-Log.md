## Active investigations

### L1 - Fresh running capacity and bike-to-run cost

- Question/hypothesis: The run is the largest identified Olympic performance opportunity, but its limiting mechanism is unresolved. Fresh capacity, durability, coordination, preceding bike cost and pacing are competing explanations.
- Evidence and alternatives: The [Druskininkai review](../reviews/races/2026-09-05-druskininkai-olympic.md) records a stable first three run loops, a late surge and difficulty accessing faster sustainable pace. A 2025-12-11 through 2026-09-11 COROS review found 36 outdoor runs, 283.5 km and 31.6 hours. Across 32 comparable ordinary runs, robust heart-rate adjustment estimated pace at 150 bpm improving from 7:19 to 6:11/km; sensitivity analyses remained approximately 50-70 s/km faster, and modeled HR at 6:30/km fell about 10.5 bpm. A standardized km 2-6 comparison supported about 51 s/km improvement. Gains were clearest over km 2-5, while steady-run cardiac-drift medians were effectively flat and the recent longer-run sample was only three, so fresh short-to-moderate field efficiency improved but 10 km durability is not established. Historical half-marathon ability is not a current baseline. Measured race cadence/power is absent; assumed gearing and average speed cannot establish race cadence. The first Favero-recorded ride on 2026-09-09 showed 64 rpm overall and approximately 65 rpm through the first 20 km, including 196 W at 64 rpm over km 5-10. This supports a naturally torque-heavy pedaling pattern, but mixed surface, strong wind, interruptions, new shoes and deliberate intensity changes prevent attribution to race execution or subsequent running. Adequate-feeling effort and tolerated fueling do not exclude bike or nutritional cost. On 2026-09-16 Danielius completed 10.01 km in 1:02:34 at RPE 4/10 with at least 15 minutes of reported reserve; the second 5 km was faster while cadence, ground-contact time and vertical ratio remained stable. This supports fresh running durability over about one hour, but is not a comparable RUN-E baseline and does not establish bike-to-run cost or longer-distance durability. The first RUN-E observation on 2026-09-21 was 45:01 and 6.99 km at 6:26/km, 150 bpm average and 161 bpm maximum heart rate, with reported RPE 3/10 and a very relaxed feel. Heart rate rose from 133 bpm in km 1 to about 155-157 bpm later while pace eased from roughly 6:13-6:14/km to 6:35-6:36/km. Polar H10 use confirms that the heart-rate signal is treated as reliable. Strong wind, the prior hard bike two days earlier and ordinary warm-up/duration response remain competing explanations; one observation cannot establish abnormal drift or effort mismatch. On 2026-09-25 Danielius completed 1:10:01 and 11.42 km at reported RPE 3-4/10 with about 10 minutes of reserve and no immediate calf or Achilles symptoms. One split contains an obvious GPS anomaly, so final-15-minute pace change is not interpreted. This supports fresh aerobic durability through 70 minutes; next-day tolerance and bike-to-run durability remain unresolved.
- Intervention/decision: Use Current Season's activated RUN-E reference as the repeatable submaximal comparison and RUN-F as appropriate, then BRICK-C only if needed. Preserve the same flat route, chest strap, shoes, intended effort and important conditions; review average pace and duration response without creating a competing monthly protocol. Cadence is now measurable. Before prescribing a correction, compare natural cadence with small controlled increases at similar easy power on a flat, uninterrupted ride and observe heart rate, leg effort, right-foot response and later running. Do not jump directly to a fixed cadence target.
- Expected result and revision criterion: Comparable fresh and post-bike evidence will narrow the limiter. Relatively strong fresh running with poorer post-bike response supports transfer investigation; similarly limited performance fresh weakens bike cost as the sole explanation. Inconsistent conditions leave the result unresolved. Change one major variable in a later trial.
- Review trigger: First relevant activated benchmark pair, then the end of the first adopted development block; earlier if reliable cadence or contradictory evidence arrives.
- Status/outcome: unresolved; fresh submaximal field efficiency and aerobic durability through 70 minutes are supported subfindings. RUN-E still has only one effort-valid and sensor-valid but environmentally noisy observation; repeatability, longer-distance durability beyond 70 minutes, bike-to-run cost and the specific speed limiter remain unresolved.
- Confidence: high for improved fresh short-to-moderate field efficiency; low for a specific durability, physiological or gearing mechanism.

### L2 - Session purpose, ceiling and sustainable execution

- Question/hypothesis: Explicit success criteria and ceilings, combined with better prescription fit, may reduce unintended session escalation while preserving enjoyment and key-session quality.
- Evidence and alternatives: Prior records show multiple short/easy prescriptions becoming longer or harder, including the 2026-08-26 bike, 2026-08-28 swim, race-week run and the 2026-09-11 first post-race run, where a 20-minute 2-3/10 impact-tolerance test became 39:38 at 4/10 with a 5:35 kilometre. Across 2026-07-20 through 2026-09-13 Danielius recorded 43 sessions and about 43.7 hours, averaging about 5.5 hours per week, with several higher-volume weeks tolerated. On 2026-09-13 he explicitly reported that coaching had become overprotective and too consistently slower/shorter despite 4-6 available hours and a performance goal. Immediate tolerance does not prove unlimited capacity, but the pattern supports underestimated prescription as a real alternative to non-adherence. In the first block week, Danielius completed six sessions in six days and approximately 6 hours 12 minutes, slightly above the normal 6-hour maximum. The controlled-fast run reached RPE 8/10 instead of 6-7/10 and the key bike intervals reached RPE 7/10 instead of 6/10, while the 62-minute long run was well tolerated with reserve and no disruptive calf response was reported. On 2026-09-23 the same 4 x 4-minute fast-run dose was repeated with clearer execution: reported RPE 7/10, one-repetition reserve, stable pace and power through the fourth repetition, and no finishing test. This supports adequate prescribed exposure and suggests that repeating the dose with an explicit ceiling improved control. The almost absent cooldown and still-pending next-day response mean the execution finding is promising rather than closed. The following day's quality swim was completed within its time ceiling, but Danielius reported RPE 6/10 versus the intended 5/10 and declining energy after two of four work repetitions; pace also slowed over the work set. The shoulder remained okay. The preceding fast run and omission of a separate easy swim warm-up confound attribution, but the result shows that next-day quality was not fully preserved. The 2026-09-25 long-run progression then matched its 70-minute ceiling and RPE 3-4/10 target, retained about 10 minutes of reserve and produced no immediate calf or Achilles symptoms. This further supports prescription fit while delayed recovery remains unobserved.
- Intervention/decision: Use the Coach Rules session contract and the new progression/underdosing guardrail. The confirmed 2026-09-14 block defaults to its target exposure and includes meaningful quality plus progression. Explain ceilings, but treat repeated favorable overshoot as evidence to recalibrate future planned dose. Keep reporting concise and evaluate execution plus downstream response at weekly reviews and block closure.
- Expected result and revision criterion: Target exposure is normally prescribed and achieved, quality sessions and planned progression occur, next key sessions remain feasible, and reporting/enjoyment are acceptable. If recovery or symptoms deteriorate, reduce the affected dose. If the athlete still experiences the plan as chronically conservative despite favorable response, strengthen progression or revise the block design rather than adding more return tests.
- Review trigger: First weekly review containing relevant sessions and end of the first adopted block.
- Status/outcome: partially supported. The first week showed sufficient volume, meaningful quality and progression rather than underdosing. The 2026-09-23 controlled-fast repeat matched the prescribed RPE and retained one-repetition reserve without late fade, supporting improved dose control. The next-day quality swim was completed but lost energy and pace after two work repetitions, so preservation of subsequent-session quality remains mixed rather than confirmed.
- Confidence: medium for the observed pattern and low-to-medium for the intervention effect until repeated and reviewed.

### L3 - Rolling queue and state architecture

- Question/hypothesis: A rolling queue fits variable family availability; separating knowledge by ownership should prevent stale-state errors.
- Evidence and alternatives: The queue was used across moved/modified sessions and remains the confirmed preference. No controlled comparison establishes improved adherence. The 2026-09-07 audit found old telemetry and expired directions accumulating in Active State despite the 2026-07-25 refactor. The 2026-09-12 pre-change validator again found daily telemetry and excessive size in Active State, while the planned 4-6 week learning cycle had no explicit current block contract or executable closure state.
- Intervention/decision: Retain the rolling queue and canonical routing. Keep one compact current block contract in Active State, let the existing weekly review enter block-review mode only when its trigger is due, and keep it as the sole scheduled planner. Persist durable learning in place and remove completed operational detail instead of creating monthly report files or a parallel task.
- Expected result and revision criterion: One confirmed queue maps to one current block contract; due reviews compare expected with observed response and produce bounded learning before the next proposal. No contradictory active queue, parallel planner, expired instructions or recurring telemetry accumulation should appear. Recurrence, excessive reading burden or a review that does not change or justify retaining a decision weakens implementation effectiveness.
- Review trigger: Every structural validation and the first block review; revisit sooner if an inconsistency affects a decision.
- Status/outcome: weakened for the prior assumption that file separation alone ensured clean state; practical queue suitability is supported by preference/use, not a causal adherence result.
- Confidence: high for the observed architecture defect and confirmed queue preference; low for quantified adherence gains.

- Question/hypothesis: Small frequent intakes and pre-swim carbohydrate can be practically tolerated; performance benefit and optimal fluid/sodium doses are separate questions.
- Evidence and alternatives: Early July/August trials tolerated smaller doses. The [race review](../reviews/races/2026-09-05-druskininkai-olympic.md) documents the higher race intake without gastrointestinal problems. Palatability of the earlier pre-swim gel was poor. Changes in readiness, prior food and expectation confound reported energy benefits; fluid loss was not measured.
- Intervention/decision: Retain the tolerated intake rhythm. The old instructions to complete a pre-race higher-dose rehearsal are closed. Use race evidence as an experience reference, not a universal prescription for longer/hotter events; assess fuel/fluid/sodium needs for each new context.
- Expected result and revision criterion: Continued practical tolerance; revise the product/dose/timing if symptoms, palatability or late-session response change. No gastrointestinal symptoms alone does not prove adequate hydration or optimal fueling.
- Review trigger: Next materially longer/different-condition rehearsal or a tolerance problem.
- Status/outcome: supported for practical race tolerance; unresolved for isolated performance benefit and optimal hydration/sodium. Prior dose-rehearsal trials are retired.
- Confidence: high for reported race tolerance; low for isolated causal performance claims and individual fluid/sodium sufficiency.
