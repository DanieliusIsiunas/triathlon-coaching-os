### Activation and comparison rules

- The current recovery queue and symptom gates take precedence. No baseline test until progressive return has been tolerated, current relevant COROS and subjective inputs are available, and the test is included in a confirmed queue. New pain, illness or disrupted recovery defers it.
- Fit measurements into ordinary training; do not add all protocols as extra sessions. Start with a fresh-running reference; add one comparison only when it answers a remaining decision. No mandatory maximal tests.
- At activation record the exact route/pool length, equipment, warm-up, recovery between repetitions, effort target, preceding 48-hour training, relevant weather, fueling and timing. Repeat comparable conditions. A materially modified protocol establishes a new baseline, not a clean improvement.
- Retain only the protocol identity and durable baseline/change conclusion with dates, uncertainty and decision in the Learning Log. COROS owns activity/lap detail. Never invent missing results.

### Protocol menu

Rate of Perceived Exertion (RPE) uses a 1-10 scale.

| Protocol | Standardized design once activated | Decision and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| RUN-E: easy reference | Same flat measured route; 10 minutes easy warm-up, 20 minutes at conversational effort around RPE 3/10, 5 minutes easy finish. Record output and early/late effort and reliable heart rate. | Establish easy-running repeatability and duration response. Weather, sensor quality and chosen pace confound heart-rate drift; this is not a threshold or race prediction test. |
| RUN-F: fresh performance reference | After normal training is tolerated and a fresh benchmark is justified: same flat measured 5 km, 15-minute easy warm-up, even controlled effort around RPE 7/10 with ceiling 8/10 and no finishing sprint, 5-10 minute easy finish. Record splits and effort. | Estimate current controlled fresh capability. Repeat the same effort/protocol; do not equate it to an all-out personal best, a measured lactate threshold or automatic 10 km prediction. Defer when its dose is inappropriate. |
| BRICK-C: controlled comparison | Use RUN-E's 20-minute main portion as the comparison segment. Precede it with 30 minutes easy cycling after 10 minutes easy bike warm-up; transition within 5 minutes; finish with 5 minutes easy running. Hold bike setup, route/indoor resistance, effort around RPE 3/10 and transition constant. | Compare running response at the same intended easy effort with RUN-E; acknowledge different warm-up/preload. This estimates short-bike transition cost, not full race durability. Later extend bike duration only through ordinary confirmed progression. |
| SWIM-T: technique reference | Same pool, 200 m easy warm-up, 4 x 200 m at controlled RPE 4/10 with 20-second rests, 100 m easy finish. One technical cue; no new paddles or deliberate cadence target. Record repeat pace, perceived technique and symptoms. | Establish repeatability of controlled technique. Introduce continuous/open-water transfer separately when safe; easy repetition success alone does not validate race pace. |

These initial doses are comparison designs, not minimum weekly requirements. At activation the coach must choose a lower-risk alternative if a design exceeds current tolerance; document the new baseline rather than forcing standardization.

Review after one completed development block, provisionally 4-6 weeks from its actual start, or earlier when meaningful evidence changes the decision. Repeat rather than escalate if results are noisy. Seek improvement beyond the individual's observed variation with acceptable recovery on more than one comparable observation; there is no invented universal percentage threshold. If capability improves fresh but not after cycling, investigate transfer and bike cost. If neither improves, review execution, dose, recovery and measurement before adding intensity. A fresh 5 km result alone cannot validate Project 2:49.
